## Mechanism study (pre-declared cuts)
- **Frequency (H-selection): DEAD.** Killer teams lead band spots at the identical rate
  (6.4 vs 6.3 per team-season).
- **Live tells: DEAD.** At spot time the two lead classes are indistinguishable — leader eFG
  edge +5.5 vs +6.4pp, MC 0.731 vs 0.735, similar 3PM/paint/FTA. Weak leans only: killer leads
  more often 3PT-built (56% vs 41%), killer leaders sloppier (TO −1.4 vs −0.6).
- **Faster shooting decay: DEAD.** Both classes' shooting edges mean-revert equally by late
  game (−11.7 vs −12.9pp). The difference is not in the fade of the lead.
- **Scalp anatomy (n=26): CONFIRMED two-sided variance.** Bad teams' elite wins = own heat
  (+3.9pp over norm) AND elite opponent cold (−3.7pp under norm). Scalps mark teams whose wins
  require coincidence. (Manny's "extenuating circumstances" hypothesis: half right.)
- **Where the effect lives:** gap .15–.30 → killer 75% (n=24) vs no-scalp 46% (n=26); gap >.30
  → flat (57/56). Non-elite trailers → 76 vs 45; elite trailers → flat-to-flipped (55/61,
  small n). Confounds cleared: no-scalp cell actually has LARGER gaps (.366 vs .324) and equal
  trailer quality — the edge is not trailer-driven.

**Synthesis:** when the trailer is overwhelming, leader profile is irrelevant. In MARGINAL
spots — modest gap, good-not-elite trailer — the live question is "is this bad team's lead
real tonight?" A no-scalp bad team leading there often IS having a genuinely better night
(46% ≈ coin flip, leads slightly more paint/FT-built). A killer team's identical-looking lead
collapses 75% — season profile says their wins need aligned variance, and it un-aligns.
The late-game channel (closing execution vs shooting) is NOT identified — open question,
possibly testable against clutch_profiles.
